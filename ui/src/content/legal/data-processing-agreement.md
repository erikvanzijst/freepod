# Freepod — Data Processing Agreement (DPA)

This Data Processing Agreement ("**DPA**") forms part of the Freepod Terms of
Service (the "**Terms**") between Freepod ("**Freepod**",
"**Processor**") and the customer ("**Customer**", "**Controller**"), and applies
where Freepod processes Personal Data contained in Customer Content on the
Customer's behalf.

In case of conflict between this DPA and the Terms, this DPA prevails with
respect to the processing of Personal Data.

**Effective date:** 2026-09-27  
This DPA takes effect on the effective date of the Terms and is versioned
alongside them.

---

## 1. Definitions

Terms such as "**Personal Data**", "**Processing**", "**Controller**",
"**Processor**", "**Data Subject**", "**Personal Data Breach**", and
"**Supervisory Authority**" have the meanings given in the **GDPR** (Regulation
(EU) 2016/679). "**Data Protection Law**" means the GDPR and any other applicable
data-protection law. "**Sub-processor**" means any processor engaged by Freepod
to process Personal Data on the Customer's behalf.

---

## 2. Roles and scope

2.1 The Customer is the **Controller** (or a processor acting on behalf of a
third-party controller) of Personal Data within Customer Content. Freepod is the
**Processor**.

2.2 Freepod processes Personal Data only to provide the Service and as set out in
**Annex 1** (subject matter, duration, nature and purpose, types of Personal
Data, and categories of Data Subjects).

2.3 The Customer is responsible for the lawfulness of the Personal Data it
provides and for having a valid legal basis for the processing.

2.4 **Third-Party Applications.** Where the Customer deploys a third-party or
open-source application through the Service ("**Third-Party Application**"), the
Customer is the **Controller** (or a processor acting for a third-party
controller) of Personal Data that the application processes, and Freepod acts as
**Processor** (or Sub-processor) limited to hosting the application. Freepod does
not author, determine the purposes or means of, or otherwise control the
application's processing — including any cookies it sets, telemetry it transmits,
or Personal Data it collects, stores, or discloses. The Customer is solely
responsible for selecting and lawfully configuring the application and for
providing any notices and obtaining any consents required from the application's
users.

2.5 **Sign in with Freepod.** Where the Customer enables Sign in with Freepod
for an application it deploys, Freepod authenticates the application's users
against their Freepod accounts and, after each user has consented through
Freepod's consent screen, discloses to the application that user's name, email
address and a stable account identifier. For that authentication and
disclosure Freepod acts as an independent **Controller**, not as the Customer's
Processor, and this DPA does not govern it. On receipt, the Customer becomes an
independent **Controller** of the disclosed data and shall:

(a) process it only to operate the application for the user who signed in, and
in accordance with Data Protection Law;

(b) provide the application's users with its own privacy notice covering that
processing, and handle their data-subject requests in respect of it;

(c) not attempt to obtain, infer, or forge the identity of a user other than
through what Freepod discloses for a sign-in that user completed; and

(d) not represent itself as Freepod, or its application as operated or endorsed
by Freepod, to the application's users.

A user's withdrawal of consent stops future disclosures to the application; it
does not oblige Freepod to recover data already disclosed.

## 3. Processor obligations (Article 28(3))

Freepod will:

3.1 **Documented instructions.** Process Personal Data only on the Customer's
documented instructions, including the Terms, this DPA, and use of the Service's
features, unless required to act by EU or Member-State law (in which case Freepod
will inform the Customer unless legally prohibited). Freepod will inform the
Customer if it considers an instruction to infringe Data Protection Law.

3.2 **Confidentiality.** Ensure that persons authorised to process the Personal
Data are bound by confidentiality obligations.

3.3 **Security (Article 32).** Implement appropriate technical and organisational
measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, as described in
**Annex 2**.

3.4 **Sub-processors.** Comply with Section 4.

3.5 **Assistance with Data Subject requests.** Taking into account the nature of
the processing, assist the Customer by appropriate technical and organisational
measures, insofar as possible, to respond to requests by Data Subjects exercising
their rights (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability,
objection).

3.6 **Assistance with compliance (Articles 32–36).** Assist the Customer in
ensuring compliance with its obligations regarding security, breach notification,
data-protection impact assessments, and prior consultation, taking into account
the nature of processing and the information available to Freepod.

3.7 **Personal Data Breach (Articles 33–34).** Notify the Customer **without
undue delay** after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting the
Customer's Personal Data, and provide information reasonably available to assist
the Customer in meeting its own notification obligations.

3.8 **Deletion or return.** At the Customer's choice, delete or return all
Personal Data after the end of the provision of the Service, and delete existing
copies, unless EU or Member-State law requires storage. This aligns with the
Export Window and deletion terms in the Terms. A deleted deployment's database
and object storage are destroyed after the retention period stated in the Terms
(Section 5.5); the Processor operates no tenant-reachable backup or restore
facility, so return of Personal Data must be exported by the Customer before
deletion.

3.9 **Audits and information.** Make available to the Customer information
necessary to demonstrate compliance with Article 28, and allow for and
contribute to audits, including inspections, conducted by the Customer or an
auditor it mandates, subject to reasonable confidentiality, security, frequency,
and notice conditions set out in Section 6.

## 4. Sub-processors

4.1 **General authorisation.** The Customer grants Freepod general authorisation
to engage Sub-processors. Freepod's current Sub-processors are listed in
**Annex 3** (or at `[SUB-PROCESSOR LIST URL]`).

4.2 **Changes.** Freepod will inform the Customer of any intended addition or
replacement of Sub-processors with at least 30 days' notice, giving the
Customer the opportunity to object on reasonable data-protection grounds. If the
Customer objects and the parties cannot resolve it, the Customer may terminate
the affected Service.

4.3 **Flow-down.** Freepod will impose on each Sub-processor, by contract, data-
protection obligations no less protective than those in this DPA, and remains
liable to the Customer for the Sub-processor's performance of those obligations.

---

## 5. International transfers

5.1 Freepod will not transfer Personal Data outside the EEA except in compliance
with Data Protection Law.

5.2 Where Personal Data is transferred to a country without an adequacy decision,
the transfer will be governed by an appropriate safeguard, in particular the **EU
Standard Contractual Clauses** (Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914),
which are incorporated by reference and completed as set out in **Annex 4**,
together with any supplementary measures required.

---

## 6. Audit conditions

6.1 Audits under Section 3.9 will occur no more than once per year (except
where required by a Supervisory Authority or following a Personal Data Breach),
on at least 30 days' written notice, during business hours, without unduly
disrupting Freepod's operations, and subject to confidentiality undertakings.

6.2 Freepod may satisfy audit requests in the first instance by providing
relevant certifications, third-party audit reports (e.g. ISO 27001 / SOC 2),
or completed questionnaires.

---

## 7. Liability and term

7.1 Liability under this DPA is subject to the limitations in the Terms, **except**
that nothing limits either party's liability to Data Subjects or under Data
Protection Law where such limitation is not permitted.

7.2 This DPA takes effect on the effective date of the Terms and continues for as
long as Freepod processes Personal Data on the Customer's behalf.

## Annex 1 — Details of processing

- **Roles:** The Customer is the Controller (or, where the Customer is itself a
  processor for a third-party controller, the Customer is a processor and Freepod
  is a Sub-processor). Freepod is the Processor.
- **Subject matter:** Freepod's hosting and operation of the Customer's
  Instance(s) and any deployed Third-Party Applications, and the storage and
  related processing of Personal Data contained therein, as necessary to provide
  the Service.
- **Duration:** For the term of the Terms and until deletion or return of the
  Personal Data in accordance with Section 3.8 (the Export Window and routine
  backup cycles).
- **Frequency:** Continuous, for as long as the Instance exists.
- **Nature of processing:** Storage, hosting, hosting-related computation,
  transmission, caching, backup, replication, restoration, deletion, and security
  and operational monitoring of Personal Data, performed at the Customer's
  direction. Freepod does not access the content of the Personal Data except as
  necessary to provide, secure, or support the Service, or as required by law.
- **Purpose of processing:** To provide the Service — that is, to host and operate
  the Customer's chosen application Instance(s) and store the associated data on
  the Customer's behalf.
- **Categories of Data Subjects (determined by the Customer):** may include, for
  example, the Customer's own customers, clients, members, or subscribers; the
  Customer's employees, contractors, and contacts; visitors and end users of the
  Customer's deployed Third-Party Applications; and any other individuals whose
  Personal Data the Customer or its End Users choose to store or process through
  the Service.
- **Types of Personal Data (determined by the Customer):** may include, for
  example, identification and contact details (e.g. names, email addresses,
  usernames, phone numbers, postal addresses); authentication data for the
  Customer's own applications; content within files, documents, images, audio, or
  video; communications and message content; and any other Personal Data the
  Customer or its End Users choose to store. Freepod does not control or
  predetermine which categories are uploaded.
- **Special-category and criminal-offence data (Articles 9 and 10):** The Service
  is **not specifically designed or marketed for** special-category Personal Data
  (Article 9) or criminal-offence data (Article 10). The Customer may
  nevertheless technically store such data within its Instance (for example, a
  photo or document may reveal it). The Customer is **solely responsible** for
  determining the lawfulness of any such processing, for having a valid Article 9
  or Article 10 basis, and for implementing any additional safeguards required.
- **Retention:** Personal Data is retained while it remains in the Customer's
  Instance and during the Export Window after termination, after which it is
  deleted in accordance with Section 3.8 and routine backup cycles, unless
  applicable law requires longer retention. A deleted deployment's database and
  object storage are destroyed after the retention period in the Terms
  (Section 5.5), currently 1 day.

## Annex 2 — Technical and organisational measures (Article 32)

- **Access control:** role-based access, least privilege, MFA for administrative
  access.
- **Encryption:** encryption in transit (TLS); encryption at rest where
  implemented.
- **Network security:** firewalling, isolation between customer Instances,
  intrusion monitoring.
- **Resilience:** backups, disaster-recovery procedures, monitoring.
- **Operational security:** logging, change management, vulnerability management,
  patching.
- **Personnel:** confidentiality agreements, security awareness.
- **Sub-processor management:** contractual flow-down and review.
- **Incident response:** documented breach-detection and response process.
